"There are, it is alleged, certain beliefs, so useful, not to say indispensable to well-being, that it is as much the duty of governments to uphold those beliefs, as to protect any other of the interests of society."
— John Stuart Mill, On Liberty

Mill asks a philosophical question about why freedom of expression matters. This research moves from that philosophical question to a sociological one: how do people actually understand their legal freedom of expression?

Positioning

Why interpretivism fits

  • The question is about understanding how people make sense of a contested concept across two different period frames — a comparison, not a causal test.
  • It's clearly a meaning-making question, aiming to understand freedom of expression from the inside, not to explain it from the outside.
4 / 13
Contribution

Why is the question important?

It is not just asking whether freedom of expression exists in Kazakhstan. It asks how people who work in politically sensitive areas — journalism, activism — understand what the right means and where its limits are.

Legal consciousness & political context

Horak, Lacko & Klocek (2020)
Helped derive legal consciousness as a key concept for this research.
Kubal (2024)
Connects legal consciousness directly to political change.
Neundorf & Pop-Eleches (2020)
Gives reason to think the political context people experience as they come of age may have lasting, distinct effects.

The gap: we don't yet know how these different political experiences are reflected in how Kazakhstani journalists and activists interpret freedom of expression across periods.

Analytical framework

Main analytical lens: legal consciousness

Legal consciousness — a complex of law-related knowledge, skills, attitudes, beliefs, and values of an individual, whereby the mutual relationship between the individual and law is being created, deepened, and developed within the context of a specific society and legal system, providing that system with the necessary authority and legitimacy for the regulation of human behavior.
— Horak, Lacko & Klocek (2020)
